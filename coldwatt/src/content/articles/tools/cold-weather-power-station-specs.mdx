## How to read this table

**Lowest charging temperature** is the one that catches people out. Lithium batteries — including the LiFePO4 chemistry used in most modern power stations — can be damaged if they're charged while frozen, so most units block charging below about 0°C. In practice, that means a power station left in a cold garage or truck can't be topped up by solar panels or a wall outlet until it warms up.

**Lowest operating temperature** is how cold it can get while the unit still powers your devices. Below that, the battery management system will usually shut the unit off to protect the cells.

Neither number is a hard cliff. Even above the minimum, a cold battery delivers noticeably less energy than a warm one. Our [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/) factors that in.

## What this means for real winter use

- **Home backup:** keep the power station in the heated part of the house. Run an extension cord to the fridge or a properly installed furnace inlet rather than moving the unit into the cold.
- **Cabins:** if the cabin cools down between visits, the power station will too. Plan to warm the unit before charging, or look for a battery with a built-in heater.
- **Winter camping and vehicles:** insulate the unit (a cooler or insulated bag works) and bring it inside the tent or cab at night. Never cover vents while it's running.
