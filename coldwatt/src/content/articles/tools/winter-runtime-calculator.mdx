## How to get accurate numbers

The defaults above are typical values, not your values. Two minutes of checking will make the estimate much better:

- **Furnace blower:** look for the sticker inside the furnace's front panel. It lists the blower motor's amps. Multiply amps by 120 to get watts (a 4 A motor ≈ 480 W). If the label says "ECM" or "variable speed," your blower probably draws far less.
- **Fridge:** the sticker inside the door or on the back lists amps or watts. That number is the compressor's peak; it only runs part of the time, which is what the "% on" column captures.
- **Everything else:** the power brick or nameplate usually lists watts or amps.

## Why the battery's temperature changes everything

Lithium batteries give back less energy when they're cold. A power station sitting in a −20°C garage might deliver only around two-thirds of what it would indoors — and some units stop working entirely below their rated minimum. Jackery, for example, rates the Explorer 1000 v2 to operate down to −10°C and to charge only at 0°C or warmer.

The practical rule: **keep the power station in the heated part of the house** and run a heavy-duty extension cord to whatever needs power.

## What the calculator doesn't know

It can't account for your battery's age, how warm the unit was before the outage, or startup surges from motors. Treat the result as a planning estimate and build in a margin — if the calculator says 10 hours, plan for 7 or 8.
