## Who it's for

The Elite 30 V2 is for people who want **a small, inexpensive safety net**: enough power to keep phones charged, a router online, a few lights on, or a CPAP running overnight during an outage. It's also handy as a second unit — keep the big power station on the furnace and fridge, and this one by the bed.

If you need to power a furnace blower or a fridge for hours, look at the [EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-3-plus/) or [Jackery Explorer 1000 v2](/power-stations/jackery-explorer-1000-v2/) instead.

## How it handles winter

**Size sets the limits.** 288 Wh goes a long way for small electronics and very little way for anything with a motor or heating element. A furnace blower drawing a few hundred watts would drain it quickly.

**Keep it warm.** BLUETTI doesn't list cold-weather limits on its product page. Treat it like any lithium power station: use it in a heated room, and bring it to room temperature before charging. Here's [why cold charging is a problem](/power-stations/can-you-charge-lifepo4-below-freezing/).

**Fast top-ups help.** BLUETTI says it charges to 80% in around 40 minutes. If power comes back briefly during a storm, that's a useful way to refill it quickly.

A capable little backup for the essentials, not a whole-home solution. Estimate what it can run in the [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/), or see all our picks in the [best power stations for cold weather](/power-stations/best-portable-power-station-cold-weather/).
