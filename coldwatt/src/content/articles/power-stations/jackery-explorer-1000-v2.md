## Who it's for

Pick the Explorer 1000 v2 if you want **a straightforward backup unit that lives indoors** — to keep the fridge, lights, internet and phones going through an outage — and you value a lighter unit you can easily move between rooms or take camping in the warmer months.

If your priority is running bigger loads or expanding capacity later, the [EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-3-plus/) is the more flexible choice.

## How it handles winter

**It's one of the few models with published cold limits.** Jackery lists an operating range of −10°C to 45°C and a charging range of 0°C to 45°C. That transparency is useful — it tells you exactly where the line is.

**What −10°C means in practice.** In much of Canada, an unheated garage, shed, cabin or vehicle will drop below −10°C on a winter night. Left there, the Explorer 1000 v2 will likely shut down. Keep it in the heated part of your home and run an extension cord where you need power.

**Capacity for outages.** With 1,070 Wh and 1,500 W of output, it can run a fridge and essentials for a good stretch, or a furnace blower for a few hours. Check your own loads in the [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/) — try setting the location to "cold cabin" to see how quickly it hits its limit.

**UPS for sensitive gear.** A 20 ms or faster switchover keeps most electronics running through a grid drop.

## Bottom line

A good indoor outage backup and a lighter camping companion, with honest cold ratings that tell you to keep it warm.
