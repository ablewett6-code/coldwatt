## Who it's for

The DELTA 3 Plus makes the most sense if your main worry is **keeping the house running through a winter outage** — furnace blower, fridge, lights, internet — and you want room to grow. It's also a good fit for a cabin with power tools or appliances that need real output.

It's less suited to carrying around. At 27.6 lb it's manageable, but if portability matters more than output, the [Jackery Explorer 1000 v2](/power-stations/jackery-explorer-1000-v2/) is lighter with similar capacity.

## How it handles winter

**Output is its strength.** Furnace blowers can draw several hundred watts with a startup surge on top. With 1,800 W continuous and 3,600 W surge, the DELTA 3 Plus has headroom to run a blower and a fridge at the same time.

**The UPS function matters more in winter than people think.** When the grid drops, a switchover under 10 ms means devices plugged into it — like a furnace control board or a modem — typically stay on without rebooting.

**Capacity is the limit.** 1,024 Wh sounds like a lot, but a furnace blower cycling on and off can use a few hundred watt-hours an hour. Expect hours of runtime, not days, unless you add batteries. Run your numbers in the [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/).

**Cold limits are the unknown.** EcoFlow's product page doesn't list charging or operating temperatures. Until the company publishes them, treat it like any lithium power station: keep it in a heated space, and never charge it while it's below freezing. See [why that matters](/power-stations/can-you-charge-lifepo4-below-freezing/).

## Solar charging in winter

Two solar inputs totalling up to 1,000 W give it a real advantage when daylight is short — you can connect more panels to make the most of a few good hours. Remember that the battery needs to be above freezing to accept a charge.

If you're buying one power station mainly to protect a home through winter outages, the DELTA 3 Plus is the strongest all-rounder we cover.
