## The key differences

These two sit in the same size class — roughly 1,000 Wh — but they're built with different priorities.

**Output.** The EcoFlow's 1,800 W continuous output is 300 W more than the Jackery's 1,500 W, and its 3,600 W surge helps with motor startups like a furnace blower or fridge compressor. If you plan to run several things at once, that headroom matters.

**Weight.** The Jackery weighs 24.2 lb against the EcoFlow's 27.6 lb. Not a huge gap, but noticeable if you'll carry it to a campsite or up cabin stairs.

**Cold-weather transparency.** Jackery lists an operating minimum of −10°C and a charging minimum of 0°C. EcoFlow's product page doesn't list either. That doesn't mean the EcoFlow is worse in the cold — it means you have less information. Either way, keep it indoors in winter. [Here's why charging in the cold is a problem](/power-stations/can-you-charge-lifepo4-below-freezing/).

**Growing later.** The DELTA 3 Plus supports add-on batteries. If you might want a full day of furnace backup down the road, that's a real advantage. Jackery's Canadian product page doesn't list expansion for this model.
