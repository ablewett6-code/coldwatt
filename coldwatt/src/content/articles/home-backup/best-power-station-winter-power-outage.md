## Match the power station to your outage plan

The right size depends on what you're trying to keep running:

| Goal | Output needed | Capacity to aim for |
|---|---|---|
| Phones, lights, router | 300 W+ | 250–500 Wh |
| Add a CPAP overnight | 300 W+ | 300–500 Wh |
| Add a fridge | 1,000 W+ | 1,000 Wh+ |
| Add a furnace blower | 1,500 W+ | 1,000 Wh+, expandable ideal |

These are starting points. Your actual needs depend on your appliances — the [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/) will give you a better estimate in two minutes.

## Before you buy: two winter essentials

**Plan where it will live.** In winter, a power station belongs in the heated part of your home. Most won't charge below 0°C and some stop working around −10°C. Check each model in the [cold-weather spec database](/tools/cold-weather-power-station-specs/).

**Plan how the furnace connects.** Most furnaces are hard-wired and need a transfer switch or inlet installed by an electrician before a power station can run them. Read our guide to [running a furnace on a power station](/home-backup/power-station-furnace-power-outage/).
