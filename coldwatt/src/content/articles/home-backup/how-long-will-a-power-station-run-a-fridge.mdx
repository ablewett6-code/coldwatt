**Short answer:** a 1,000 Wh power station kept indoors will run a typical fridge for roughly **15–20 hours**. A small 300 Wh unit manages around **4–5 hours**. The exact number depends on your fridge, and you can work it out in two minutes using a number that's already printed on it.

## Step 1: Find your fridge's EnerGuide number

Every fridge sold new in Canada comes with a yellow-and-black **EnerGuide** label showing its annual energy use in kilowatt-hours (kWh) per year. If the sticker's gone, search your fridge's model number plus "EnerGuide" or check the manual.

That annual figure already accounts for the compressor cycling on and off, so it's a far better guide than the wattage on the back of the fridge.

## Step 2: Turn it into a daily number

Divide the annual kWh by 365 to get kWh per day, then multiply by 1,000 for watt-hours:

> **400 kWh/year ÷ 365 ≈ 1.1 kWh per day ≈ 1,100 Wh per day**
>
> That's an average of about **46 W**, around the clock.

## Step 3: Work out what your power station can really deliver

A power station never delivers its full rated capacity to a fridge. Two things eat into it:

- **Reserve:** the battery management system holds back some capacity. We assume 90% is usable.
- **Inverter losses:** converting battery power to household AC wastes energy. We assume 85% efficiency.

So usable energy ≈ rated capacity × 0.9 × 0.85.

## Step 4: Divide

**Hours of runtime = usable energy ÷ average fridge draw.** Here's how that works out for the models we cover, using the 400 kWh/year example fridge, with each unit kept in a heated room:

| Power station | Rated capacity | Usable (approx.) | Fridge runtime (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 | 288 Wh | 220 Wh | ~4.8 hours |
| EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus | 1,024 Wh | 783 Wh | ~17 hours |
| Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 | 1,070 Wh | 818 Wh | ~18 hours |

Rated capacities are from each manufacturer's spec sheet. These are planning estimates, not measurements — build in a margin.

## Make it last much longer

**Cycle the fridge.** Health Canada says an unopened fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours. Instead of running it nonstop, you can run it for a stretch, unplug it, and plug it back in later. That can more than double how long a power station lasts. A cheap fridge thermometer lets you keep it at or below 4°C, the temperature Health Canada recommends.

**Keep the doors shut.** Every opening lets cold air out and makes the compressor work harder.

**Leave the freezer alone.** A full freezer keeps food frozen for about 48 hours on its own, and a half-full one about 24. Prioritize the fridge.

**Keep the power station warm.** Batteries deliver noticeably less energy when cold, and most won't recharge below 0°C. Keep the unit in a heated room and run a heavy-duty extension cord to the fridge. [Here's why cold charging matters](/power-stations/can-you-charge-lifepo4-below-freezing/).

## Which power station for a fridge?

For a fridge alone, any unit around **1,000 Wh with at least 1,000 W of output** handles the compressor's startup surge and gets you through a night.
