A summer outage is an inconvenience. A winter outage in Canada is a countdown: the house cools, pipes are at risk, and the food in the fridge has a few hours. Here's a practical checklist — before, during and after — so you're not working it out by flashlight.

## Before winter: get ready (do this now)

**Build a 72-hour kit.** Canadian utilities recommend being self-sufficient for at least 72 hours. BC Hydro's list includes:
- [ ] Water — **2 litres per person per day**
- [ ] A **three-day supply** of non-perishable, ready-to-eat food (and a manual can opener)
- [ ] Flashlights or headlamps and spare batteries
- [ ] Warm clothing, hats and blankets for everyone
- [ ] A battery or crank radio
- [ ] Medications and copies of prescriptions

**Plan for power.**
- [ ] Decide what you *need* to run: the furnace blower, the fridge, a sump pump, medical devices, a phone charger. Add up how long you'd need them.
- [ ] Estimate what a power station could handle with the [winter runtime calculator](/tools/winter-runtime-calculator/).
- [ ] If you rely on powered medical equipment, plan a backup power supply for it specifically.
- [ ] If you want the furnace on backup power, have a **licensed electrician install a transfer switch or inlet** before winter — not during the outage.

**Protect against carbon monoxide.**
- [ ] Test your **CO alarms** and replace batteries. Have one on every level and near bedrooms.

**Know your furnace.**
- [ ] Find the blower motor rating on the furnace's nameplate sticker so you can size backup power properly. [Here's how](/home-backup/power-station-furnace-power-outage/).

## During the outage

**First 15 minutes**
- [ ] Check whether it's just your home (breaker) or the whole street, and report it to your utility.
- [ ] **Unplug sensitive electronics and appliances** so a surge doesn't damage them when power returns. Leave one lamp switched on so you'll know when it's back.
- [ ] Start your backup power for the essentials: furnace blower first if it's connected, then the fridge.

**Keep the food safe**
- [ ] **Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed.** Health Canada says an unopened fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours, a full freezer about 48 hours, and a half-full freezer about 24 hours.
- [ ] If you have a power station, you don't have to run the fridge nonstop — run it in stretches. [Here's how long a power station runs a fridge](/home-backup/how-long-will-a-power-station-run-a-fridge/).

**Keep warm**
- [ ] Close off rooms you're not using and gather in one room, ideally on the sunny side of the house.
- [ ] Close curtains at night and open south-facing ones during the day.
- [ ] Dress in layers — including hats — and use blankets.
- [ ] **Never** use a barbecue, camp stove, patio heater or gas oven to heat the house.

**Carbon monoxide rules (non-negotiable)**
- [ ] Health Canada: never run a fuel-burning generator **indoors, in a garage or in a shed** — even with the door open.
- [ ] Keep it at least **6 metres (20 feet)** from the house, exhaust pointed away from windows and doors.
- [ ] If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside immediately and call 911.

**Protect the pipes**
- [ ] Keep as much heat in the house as possible for as long as possible.
- [ ] If the outage is long and you have to leave, CAA-Quebec advises: **close the main water valve, drain the taps, and pour non-toxic antifreeze into toilets and drains.**

**Look after your backup power**
- [ ] Keep your power station **indoors in a heated room.** Most lithium batteries won't charge below 0°C and deliver less energy when cold. See the [cold-weather limits for popular models](/tools/cold-weather-power-station-specs/).
- [ ] Recharge it whenever you can — if power flickers back, or from a generator running safely outside.

## After the power comes back

- [ ] Wait a few minutes, then plug appliances back in **one at a time** to avoid overloading circuits.
- [ ] Check fridge and freezer temperatures. When in doubt, throw it out — Health Canada's [emergency food safety page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-drinking-water-safe-emergency.html) explains what to keep and what to discard.
- [ ] Check for frozen or burst pipes before turning the water back on fully.
- [ ] **Recharge your power station** and restock anything you used from your kit.

## Should you add backup power?

If outages where you live last hours, a portable power station is the safest, simplest way to keep the fridge, lights and furnace fan going — see [power station vs generator](/home-backup/power-station-vs-generator-winter-outage/) to decide, then our picks for the [best power stations for a winter outage](/home-backup/best-power-station-winter-power-outage/).
